# Research Methodology

**Role in Semester 1:** Supporting academic subject  
**Status:** Roadmap established; detailed notes to be added as studied.

## Learning outcomes
- Turn a broad interest into a focused, answerable research question.
- Evaluate prior work and identify a defensible research gap.
- Choose a method appropriate to the question and available evidence.
- Design fair experiments with baselines, metrics, and reproducible procedures.
- Understand research ethics, validity, limitations, and citation practices.

## Roadmap
1. What research is; basic, applied, quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-method approaches.
2. Selecting and narrowing a topic; problem statement, objectives, research questions, hypotheses.
3. Literature search, source quality, paper screening, synthesis, and research-gap claims.
4. Research design, variables, sampling, measurement, and data collection.
5. Experimental design, baselines, controls, evaluation metrics, and statistical reasoning.
6. Validity and reliability; bias, confounding, data leakage, and threats to generalization.
7. Data management, reproducibility, versioning, and research documentation.
8. Research ethics, consent where applicable, privacy, authorship, plagiarism, and responsible AI use.
9. Reading and critiquing papers; interpreting results without overstating claims.
10. Proposal, timeline, risk plan, expected outcomes, and limitations.

## Research-paper review questions
- What specific question does the paper answer?
- What is the contribution relative to prior work?
- Are the data and methods appropriate for the question?
- Are baselines and evaluation protocols fair?
- Could leakage, confounding, selection bias, or measurement error explain the result?
- Are conclusions supported by the reported evidence?
- Can the experiment be reproduced from the available code, data, and details?
- What limitations remain?

## Practical deliverables
- A focused problem statement and research objectives.
- A literature matrix with verified citations.
- A research proposal with methods, evaluation, risks, and timeline.
- A reproducibility checklist for a small experiment.

Use the instructor's syllabus and rubric as the final authority for required topics and terminology. A research gap is a hypothesis to verify through reading, not a claim to make without evidence.
