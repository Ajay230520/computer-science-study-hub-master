# MFCS — Unit 1 Question Bank (Reference Only)

**Source:** Uploaded “Unit-1-assignment.pdf”, pages 1–2 contain Unit 1 questions; page 2 begins Unit 2. Questions below are prompts only, without solutions.

1. Explain the importance of the Central Limit Theorem and applications in finance or quality control.
2. A post-office queue time (T) has PDF (f(t)=c(81-t^2)) for (0\le t\le9), and 0 otherwise. Find (c) and the CDF (F(t)).
3. A factory item has defect probability 0.02. For a shipment of 10,000 items, find the expected number of defectives and the standard deviation.
4. For (X\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}) with probabilities (0.1,k,0.2,2k,0.3,k), find (k), mean and variance.
5. Among 800 families with five children each, assuming equal probabilities for boys and girls, find the expected number of families with (i) 3 boys, (ii) 5 girls, (iii) either 2 or 3 boys.
6. A person hits a target with probability (1/3). (i) With 5 shots, find the probability of hitting at least twice. (ii) Find how many shots are needed for the probability of at least one hit to exceed 90%.
7. Discuss probability distributions in computer vision.
8. Discuss probability distributions in bioinformatics.
9. Five ribbon samples have mean breaking strength 169.5 lb and sample standard deviation 5.7 lb. Test (H_0:\mu=180) against (H_1:\mu<180) at significance level 0.01, assuming a normal population.
10. Draw 3 balls from an urn with 5 white and 7 black balls; receive ₹10 per white and ₹5 per black. Find expected earnings.
11. (i) Find the number of ways to partition 8 people into committees where each person belongs to exactly one committee and each committee has at least 2 people. (ii) A loan/default dataset lists age, loan amount and default label; classify the default probability for a 48-year-old taking a ₹142,000 loan based on prior experience.
12. A batch of 850 parts contains 50 nonconforming parts. Select 2 without replacement; (X) is the number of nonconforming parts. Find the CDF and plot it.
13. Define probability distribution, probability mass function and cumulative distribution function with examples.
14. Copper-wire flaws follow a Poisson distribution with mean 2.3 per millimeter. Find: (i) exactly 2 flaws in 1 mm, (ii) 10 flaws in 5 mm, (iii) at least one flaw in 2 mm.
15. A store averages 448 shoppers per hour with standard deviation 21. For a sample of 49 hours, find the probability the sample mean is between 441 and 446; the original question supplies Z-table areas.
16. Each water sample has 10% chance of containing a pollutant, independently. For 18 samples, find the probability (i) exactly 2 contain it, (ii) at least 4 contain it.
17. A lottery chooses 6 numbers from 1–49, costs $1 and pays $2 million after taxes if won. Find expected net winnings/losses (i) once, (ii) weekly for 10 years.
18. Three percent of watches are defective. In a sample of 300, find the probability the defective proportion is between 0.02 and 0.035.
19. A box contains 4 white and 6 black balls. Draw 2; receive ₹140 per white and ₹70 per black. Find expected earnings.
20. Among 100 students, 80 passed all subjects, 10 failed one, 7 failed two and 3 failed three. Find the distribution of the number of subjects failed.
21. The hourly message count (X) has values 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 with probabilities 0.08, 0.15, 0.30, 0.20, 0.20, 0.07. Find the mean and standard deviation.
22. Four technicians service production-line breakdowns with given shares and incomplete-repair rates. Given an incomplete repair, find the probability Janet made the repair (Bayes' theorem).
23. A PDF is (f(x)=cx^2) for (0<x<3), zero otherwise. Find (c) and (P(1<X<2)).
24. A bad reaction has probability 0.001 per injection. For 2,000 people, find probabilities of exactly 3, more than 2, none, and more than 1 reaction.
25. Given a joint distribution table for (X\in\{0,1,3\}) and (Y\in\{-1,2.5,3,4.7\}), find (E(Y\mid X=1)). Use the table in the original PDF.
26. (X\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}) has probabilities (k,3k,5k,7k,9k). Find (k), (P(X<3)), (P(0<X<4)), and the CDF.
27. A PDF is (f(x)=cx) for (x>0), zero for (x\le0). Find (c) and the CDF.

**Source caution:** Question 27 as extracted is incomplete/likely missing a support bound; a density proportional to (x) over all (x>0) cannot be normalized. Check the original paper/instructor version before attempting it.
