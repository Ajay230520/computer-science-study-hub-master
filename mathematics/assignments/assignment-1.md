## 1. Expected profit
A company manufactures electronic gadgets. One out of every 50 gadgets is faulty. The company does not know which gadgets are faulty until a buyer complains. It earns ₹30 profit on each working gadget and loses ₹800 on each faulty gadget. Can it make a profit in the long term?

## 2. Choose a development project using expected value
Project A has probability 0.4 of achieving a value of $2 million and probability 0.6 of achieving $500,000. Project B has probability 0.3 of achieving $3 million and probability 0.7 of achieving $200,000. Which project should be chosen?

## 3. Total probability and Bayes' theorem: late flights
Caribou Air accounts for 65% of commercial flight arrivals; 22% of its arrivals are late. Polar Express Air accounts for 35%; 42% of its arrivals are late.
- (a) Find the probability a randomly selected commercial arrival is late.
- (b) Given that an arrival is late, find the probability it is a Caribou Air flight.

## 4. Compare conditional and unconditional probabilities
State whether (P(B\mid A)=P(B)), (P(B\mid A)>P(B)), or (P(B\mid A)<P(B)):
- (a) A: it rains on Tuesday next week; B: it rains on Wednesday next week, for the current location.
- (b) A: it rains on Tuesday next week; B: it rains on the day 10 years from today, for the current location.
- (c) For a randomly selected Canadian adult: A: the person did not finish high school; B: the person has been to prison.

## 5. Fish consumption and prostate cancer
A study followed 6,272 Swedish men for 30 years and categorized them by fish consumption and whether they developed prostate cancer. Using the table in the original PDF:

- (b) Find the probability that fish consumption was small, moderate, or large.
- (c) Find the probability the person developed prostate cancer.
- (d) Find the probability of prostate cancer given fish consumption was never/seldom.
