**Source:** Uploaded “Unit-2_Assignment.pdf”, pages 1–2. Prompts only; no answers included.

1. Compare Method of Moments and Maximum Likelihood Estimation; give suitable examples.
2. Define a random sample and explain its significance. Also, if 3% of watches are defective, find the probability that the defective proportion in a random sample of 300 lies between 0.02 and 0.035.
3. Company A machines have defect proportion 10%; Company B has 5%. Independent samples of 250 from A and 300 from B are taken. Find the probability that the difference in sample proportions is at most 0.02.
4. From samples of 400 men and 600 women, 200 men and 325 women favour a flyover. Test whether the population proportions are equal at the 5% significance level.
5. Bulb lifetime has mean 1,000 hours and standard deviation 125 hours. Determine the minimum sample size needed so 90% of bulbs do not fall short of the guaranteed average by more than 2.5%, using the normal-area information in the original question.
6. Four losses from a Gamma distribution are 200, 300, 350, 450. Find a Method of Moments estimate for parameter \(\alpha\).
7. For the discrete distribution shown in the original PDF, estimate parameter \(\theta\) by maximum likelihood from observations (3,0,2,1,3,2,1,0,2,1). The PMF formula is image-based and should be read from the original.
8. Observations 5, 9, 3, 12, 14 are from a Poisson distribution. Find the maximum likelihood estimate of \(\lambda\).
9. Given a sample from a binomial distribution, estimate (n) and (p) using Method of Moments. The source includes the PMF/table and sample list; consult the original PDF for exact formatting.
10. Population assets are 44, 34, 33 and 14 (billions). Samples of size 2 are selected with replacement. List possible samples and sample means, describe the sampling distribution of the sample mean, find its mean, and discuss whether sample means target the population mean and make good estimators.
